## #6 — Website + Google Business Profile Subscription for Local Trades

### A. Business Overview
- **What it does:** Builds and maintains a simple, fast, mobile-first website for local tradespeople (plumbers, HVAC, roofers, landscapers, cleaners, auto shops) and manages their **Google Business Profile (GBP)**: photos, posts, Q&A, review-request texts, and review replies. **$0 down, $149/month**, with a small setup fee.
- **Target customers:** Owner-operated trade businesses with 1–10 employees that have **no website, an outdated website, or a GBP with fewer than ~20 reviews**. Find them on Google Maps.
- **Problem solved:** Customers find local services on Google and choose based on reviews. Tradespeople don't have time for marketing.
- **How you make money:** A monthly subscription (recurring), plus a ~$200 setup fee.

### B. Why This Business Could Work
- **Demand driver:** Reviews and local search strongly influence local purchases. BrightLocal's consumer survey reportedly finds **~97% of consumers read reviews** before choosing a local business (as cited by [NiceJob](https://get.nicejob.com/resources/do-google-reviews-help-seo-what-the-data-actually-shows); verify against the primary report). Demand is **recurring** because profiles need ongoing activity and reviews.
- **Why you:** Agencies typically charge **$125–$500/month just for GBP management** ([Merchynt pricing guide](https://merchynt.com/post/google-my-business-management-pricing)). You bundle website + GBP at the low end, with **local, in-person service**.
- **Barriers:** Low technical barriers (modern site builders), but **high sales friction**. Cold outreach to busy tradespeople has low response rates, and the market is flooded with spammy agency offers.
- **Student fit:** Fully remote and asynchronous, so it's the **best schedule fit** on the list. Skills (web, marketing) are valuable for your careers.
- **Proven vs. your odds:** The subscription model is proven. **Your odds are weaker** because sales skill, not delivery, is the bottleneck.

## #7 — Holiday Light Installation (Lease Model)

### A. Business Overview
- **What it does:** Designs, installs, maintains, removes, and stores commercial-grade roofline and landscape holiday lighting. In the **lease model** you own the lights and reuse them for 3–5 years; the customer pays one all-in price.
- **Target customers:** Homeowners in $350K+ neighborhoods who want lights but don't want ladders. Small businesses (restaurants, boutiques) want festive storefronts.
- **Problem solved:** Ladder risk, time, storage, and "the lights never look professional."
- **How you make money:** All-in seasonal price, modeled at **$500 per home** (install + removal + light lease).

### B. Why This Business Could Work
- **Demand:** Strong but **seasonal** (installs late Oct–mid Dec, removals January). **Repeat rate is high** because customers rebook the same design each year.
- **Why you:** Fast quotes, a clean install, guaranteed takedown, and you can **sell to window and pressure-washing customers** (#1, #4).
- **Barriers:** Moderate. Ladder and roof safety, upfront inventory, and design skill.
- **Student fit: Mixed.** Peak installs overlap **Thanksgiving week through finals**, while removals land during winter break.
- **Timing note:** It's **November 9, 2026**, so this season is already underway. Launching now is possible but late, and most homeowners booked in October.

## #8 — Short-Term Rental (Airbnb/VRBO) Turnover Cleaning

### A. Business Overview
- **What it does:** Cleans and resets vacation rentals between guests: bathrooms, kitchens, linens (wash or swap), restock consumables, damage and inventory photo reports to the host.
- **Target customers:** Individual hosts with 1–5 properties and small property managers near campus, downtown, or event venues (football weekends, graduations).
- **How you make money:** Per turnover, modeled at **$95** (1–2 bedroom).

### B. Why This Business Could Work
- **Demand:** **Recurring and transaction-driven.** Every booking creates a clean. Hosts struggle to find reliable cleaners; reliability is the #1 thing they pay for.
- **Market context:** AirDNA projected **6.8% demand growth for 2025** ([BusinessWire, AirDNA 2025 Outlook](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241205094869/en/AirDNA-2025-Outlook-Report-U.S.-Short-Term-Rental-Industry-Finds-Balance)). Demand exists, but **host margins are under pressure**, so expect price pushback.
- **Student fit: Poor.** Turnovers mostly happen between ~11 a.m. check-out and ~3–4 p.m. check-in on any weekday, which collides with classes. Weekends and game days are a better fit.

## #9 — Campus Moving Labor + Summer Storage

### A. Business Overview
- **What it does:** (1) **Summer storage:** pick up students' boxes and furniture at spring move-out, store them in a rented unit, and deliver to the new dorm or apartment in August. (2) **Moving labor:** two-person labor-only moves (loading rental trucks, apartment-to-apartment moves) year-round.
- **Target customers:** Out-of-state and international students (they can't take things home), students moving between apartments, and parents (often the payer).
- **How you make money:** Storage priced per summer (modeled **$180/customer**, roughly 6–8 items), and moving labor **~$100/hr for two movers** (modeled $300/job).

### B. Why This Business Could Work
- **Demand:** **Highly seasonal and concentrated** (one week in May, one in August), but every campus has it every year.
- **Why you:** Competitors charge more. A Yale student-run service charged a flat $250, versus **"upwards of $500"** for national players ([Yale Daily News](https://yaledailynews.com/?p=189138)). You're on campus, trusted by peers, and can price per box.
- **Barriers:** Logistics, storage space, liability for customers' goods, and **campus permission** (some universities have exclusive vendor contracts).
- **Student fit:** Mixed. The peak happens exactly when you're taking finals and moving out yourselves.

## #10 — Move-Out Furniture Sourcing & Resale

### A. Business Overview
- **What it does:** Collect furniture and dorm goods (mini-fridges, desks, futons, lamps, rugs) **free or cheap during May move-out**, clean them, store them over the summer, and resell them in **August** to incoming students via Facebook Marketplace, with optional delivery.
- **Target customers:** Sellers are departing students and graduating seniors who need things gone fast. Buyers are incoming students and parents in August.
- **How you make money:** Buy low (free–$20) and sell at **$40–$150**, plus delivery fees.

### B. Why This Business Could Work (and why it's ranked last)
- **Real arbitrage:** Supply is massive and urgent in May, and demand is massive in August. The seasonal price gap is real ([BookScouter](https://bookscouter.com/blog/flipping-college-furniture/); [The Aggie on move-out waste](https://theaggie.org/2023/6/8/moo-ving-out-and-moo-ving-on)).
- **But:** It's **one cash cycle per year**, inventory and storage risk sits with you, the work is labor-heavy, and returns per hour are low. It's neither recurring nor scalable without a warehouse.

### C. Proof
