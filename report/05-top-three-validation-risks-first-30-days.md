# Part 5 — Top Three, Validation Experiments, Critical Risk Review & First 30 Days

## 6. The Three Best Options

### 🥇 Finalist 1 — Storefront + Residential Window Cleaning
- **Why it beats the alternatives:** It's the lowest cash test of any physical business (~$320), payment comes the **same day**, the storefront work is ground-level and therefore low-risk, the route work happens early morning and fits around classes, and it leads directly into higher-ticket services (house washing, holiday lights) with the **same customers, ladders, insurance, and brand**. It also works **right now**: November is holiday-shopping season, when storefronts care most about clean glass. Pet waste and pressure washing are weaker launches in winter.
- **Competitive advantages you can build:** (1) **Route density** on 2–3 walkable retail strips. (2) A **reliability promise** ("same day every cycle, or the next visit is free"). (3) **Photo proof** after every visit. (4) Cross-selling residential jobs to shop owners.
- **Likely first 10 customers:** 7–8 storefronts within one mile of campus (a café, pizza shop, nail salon, phone-repair store, boutique, insurance office, dentist, barbershop) and 2–3 homeowners (shop owners, professors, friends' parents).
- **First-month goals:** 60 walk-ins, ≥5 paid recurring storefronts, 2 residential jobs, insurance in place, 5 Google reviews, stop-time data.
- **Six-month upside (base):** ~24 storefronts + 6 homes/month ≈ **$2,900 revenue, ~$2,650 operating profit/month** (~$1,330 per partner pre-tax, ~36 hrs/partner/month). Add spring house washing and that could reach ~$4,000–$5,000/month in season (projection).
- **Most likely obstacles:** too few storefronts in walking distance; winter weather; residential ladder work; partners' schedules colliding during finals.
- **Abandon if:** fewer than **2 paid commitments from 60 qualified walk-ins** after adjusting price once, **or** the effective rate is still under **$20/hr at day 60**.

### 🥈 Finalist 2 — Pet Waste Removal Subscription
- **Why it beats the alternatives:** The most **documented** small-operator success stories (CNBC reviewed revenue documents for a $250K/year operator), true weekly **recurring revenue**, the lowest skill barrier, **the "gross factor" keeps competition low**, and it's the easiest business on the list to hand to an employee or sell when you graduate.
- **Competitive advantages:** route density, photo proof of service, disinfection protocol (a selling point for dog owners), and partnerships with groomers and vets.
- **Likely first 10 customers:** dual-income families with 1–2 dogs in fenced single-family homes in 2 neighborhoods near campus, plus 1–2 seniors and a real-estate agent's listing cleanup.
- **First-month goals (in season):** 600 door hangers, 3 partner referral sources, ≥10 recurring yards, a written service checklist.
- **Six-month upside (base):** ~35 yards ≈ **$3,300 revenue, ~$2,900 operating profit/month** (~$1,460 per partner, ~41 hrs/partner/month).
- **Most likely obstacles:** slow sign-ups in winter, missed visits during exams, the occasional unpleasant yard, local disposal rules.
- **Abandon if:** **fewer than 3 paid deposits per 600 door hangers in spring** (after testing two neighborhoods and two price points), or monthly churn stays above 10%.

### 🥉 Finalist 3 — Student-Staffed Tutoring Agency
- **Why it beats the alternatives:** $0–$650 at risk, uses an advantage you **already have** (being strong students at a college), and sessions happen in evenings and on weekends. It's also your **insurance policy**: if the service businesses fail validation, this still pays ~$35–$50/hr.
- **Competitive advantages:** local and in-person, cheaper than marketplaces, parent progress notes after every session, and a tutor bench recruited from your university's honors and education programs.
- **Likely first 10 customers:** families found through parent Facebook groups and school counselors: 6 Algebra II/Precalculus students and 4 SAT/ACT prep students.
- **First-month goals:** 3+ paying families, ~20 sessions, 3 written testimonials, 2 tutor candidates identified.
- **Six-month upside (base):** 44 partner sessions + 40 contractor sessions ≈ **$4,400 revenue, ~$3,100 operating profit/month** (~$1,550 per partner).
- **Most likely obstacles:** it stays a job (your hours cap income), contractor tutors flake, summer drop-off.
- **Abandon (the agency step) if:** by day 60 there's **no waitlist** (demand doesn't exceed your own hours). Keep tutoring personally as income, but don't build the agency.

### The designations
| Award | Pick | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Best Overall Business** | **Window cleaning (storefront + residential)** | Best balance of low capital, fast cash, schedule fit, and a clear growth path into a full exterior-services company |
| **Fastest Path to Cash Flow** | **Window cleaning** (tutoring a close second) | You can walk in, clean, and get paid on day 1. Tutoring needs a few days for parents to respond |
| **Best Long-Term Business** | **Pet waste removal** | Recurring revenue that can be sold, low-skill labor that's easy to delegate, and documented operators at $200K–$3M+ revenue |

**Recommended combination:** Run **window cleaning + tutoring now** (both cheap, both work in winter). **Pre-sell pet waste in February** for a March launch. Use window profits, not your starting capital, to buy pressure-washing gear in March. That gives you one year-round "exterior + recurring routes" company, with tutoring as a cash floor.

## 7. $200–$500 Validation Experiments

**Rule for all three:** only **money received** counts as validation (paid first visit, paid deposit, or prepaid package). Likes, survey answers, "call me later," and verbal enthusiasm count as **zero**.

### Experiment A — Window cleaning: "60 Doors in 10 Days"
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Spend (~$330)** | Storefront kit (two 14" channels + handles, strip washer, scraper, bucket, belt, towels) ~$200; 250 price sheets + 250 cards ~$60; first-month general liability ~$50–$75 (some insurers bill monthly; if not, run the test only on stores that don't require a certificate) |
| **Prospects** | **60 independent storefronts** on 2–3 commercial strips within 10 min of campus, plus **250 door hangers** in one owner-occupied neighborhood (residential offer) |
| **Offer** | Storefront: **$30/visit, every 2 weeks, no contract, pay after the first clean**, free door demo on the spot. Residential: outside-only window cleaning **$199 for up to 20 windows** (book with a $40 deposit) |
| **Measure** | Track in a spreadsheet: walk-ins, owner conversations, demos, paid first visits, second visits booked, minutes per stop, door-hanger deposits |
| **Positive validation** | **≥5 storefronts paid for a first visit and booked a second** (≥8% of 60) **and/or ≥2 paid residential deposits** |
| **Go** | ≥5 recurring storefronts + stop time ≤20 min → buy the ladders and residential kit and proceed |
| **Pivot** | 2–4 storefronts → test a different strip and a $25 price; push residential harder |
| **No-go** | ≤1 paid storefront after 60 qualified conversations → stop. Total loss ≈ $330, and the squeegee kit resells or gets used at home |

### Experiment B — Pet waste: "Two-Neighborhood Deposit Test"
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Timing** | Best run **late Feb–March 2027** (the spring rush). A November test in a cold climate will understate demand. If you run it now, halve the go threshold |
| **Spend (~$400)** | 600 door hangers ~$70; 10 yard signs ~$60; Nextdoor/Facebook local boost ~$100; scooper kit + disinfectant **bought only after the 3rd deposit** ~$170 |
| **Prospects** | **600 single-family homes with fenced yards** in 2 neighborhoods (pick them on Google Maps satellite view), **3 groomers + 2 vets** (referral cards), and one Nextdoor post per neighborhood |
| **Offer** | **$18/week for 1 dog (+$5 per extra dog)**, no contract, **first cleanup half price ($50)**, booked with a **$25 deposit** credited to the first month |
| **Measure** | Deposits per 100 hangers; deposit → recurring conversion after visit 4; cost per customer |
| **Positive validation** | **≥6 paid deposits within 14 days (1%)** and **≥4 still paying after the first month** |
| **Go** | Positive validation → full launch (another 1,000 hangers, LLC, and so on) |
| **Pivot** | 3–5 deposits → change the neighborhood or offer (biweekly $25) and retest once |
| **No-go** | ≤2 deposits across both neighborhoods in spring → stop. Loss ≈ $230 if the kit was never bought |

### Experiment C — Tutoring: "Three Paying Families in 21 Days" (plus the agency test)
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Spend (~$200)** | Domain + one-page site ~$60; boosted posts in 3 parent groups ~$80; library/community flyers ~$40; one official SAT practice book ~$20 |
| **Prospects** | 3 local parent Facebook groups, **10 high-school counselors/math teachers by email**, 2 public libraries, your own network |
| **Offer** | **$50/hr, free 20-min diagnostic**, or a **4-session prepaid pack for $190** |
| **Measure** | Inquiries → diagnostics → paid families; sessions booked per family |
| **Positive validation** | **≥3 families paying (prepaid pack or 2+ paid sessions) within 21 days** |
| **Agency go/no-go (day 60)** | **≥3 families on a waitlist** you can't serve yourselves → recruit contractor tutors. No waitlist → keep it as personal tutoring income only |
| **No-go** | 0–1 paying family after 21 days with all channels tried → local demand or price is wrong; move effort to Experiments A/B |

**Total validation spend for all three: ~$930**, which leaves **$1,000–$3,000 of your capital untouched** until something proves itself.

## 8. Challenging the Recommendations (Skeptical Investor Review)

### Window cleaning
- **Are revenues too optimistic?** Possibly. Experienced window cleaners disagree on whether storefront routes pay early, and one says it "may take years to develop" a good route ([WindowCleaner.com](https://community.windowcleaner.com/t/as-a-newbe-is-getting-into-storefront-worth-it/45640)). **Revision made:** I cut the base-case storefront count by about 25% (to 24 accounts at month 6) and kept residential jobs as a meaningful share of profit.
- **Hidden costs?** Insurance can cost more than the $50/month average for work at heights. Some commercial clients require certificates naming them as additional insured. Also budget for **vehicle wear and gas** (folded into variable costs at 5–6%, which may be light if your route is spread out).
- **Overcrowded?** Usually not at the small-storefront level. That's what the walk-in test checks.
- **Customer acquisition harder than assumed?** Walk-ins are uncomfortable. Expect 1 sale per 8–15 conversations. The test is designed around that rate.
- **Real competitive advantage?** Only reliability and density, which are real but copyable. The **moat is the route itself** once it's built.
- **Practical during a semester?** Yes. Storefront work happens 6–9 a.m. or after 5 p.m. Residential work goes on weekends.
- **Simpler alternative?** Tutoring pays a similar hourly rate with no ladders. Window cleaning wins only if you value building a **sellable, staffable** business.
- **Business or job?** A job for the first 6 months. It becomes a business once a hired student runs the route and you sell and manage.

### Pet waste removal
- **Too optimistic?** The 350-customers-in-3-months story is an **outlier**. My base case assumes 35 yards at month 6, about 10% of that pace.
- **Hidden costs?** Disposal (if local rules require hauling), mileage, and winter churn. Also check whether your auto insurance covers business use.
- **Overcrowded?** Growing awareness (podcasts, TikTok) is pulling in new entrants. **Check Google Maps first:** if 3+ active scoopers already serve your town, the opportunity shrinks.
- **Practical during a semester?** Yes, but the weekly obligation is non-negotiable, so you need a backup person by month 3.
- **Business or job?** The most delegable of the top three. Scooping is simple to train, and billing is recurring and automatic.
- **Revised view:** It ranks #1 on long-term potential but **#2 for starting now** because of winter timing.

### Tutoring agency
- **Too optimistic?** The agency step (110 contractor sessions at month 12) is the optimistic part. Many student tutoring businesses never get past the founders' own hours.
- **Hidden costs?** Background checks, the risk of misclassifying contractors, payment-processing fees, and a summer revenue drop.
- **Overcrowded?** Online platforms and AI study tools compete for basic homework help. **Local in-person test prep with accountability** is the defensible niche.
- **Business or job?** **A job unless you build the bench of tutors.** Treat the 60-day waitlist test as the dividing line.

### Cross-cutting risks your projections don't show
1. **Partnership breakdown.** This is the #1 killer of two-founder student businesses. Before spending money, **write a one-page partnership/LLC operating agreement**: ownership split, who decides what, what happens if one partner quits or graduates first, how profits are split versus reinvested, and a buyout formula.
2. **Taxes.** Set aside ~25–30% of profit. Self-employment tax applies once net earnings pass $400 in a year. Make quarterly estimated payments once profit is meaningful. Keep receipts.
3. **School and visa constraints.** International students on F-1 visas generally **cannot do off-campus self-employment work**. If either of you is on a visa, check with your international student office before launching.
4. **Vehicle.** Every service business here except tutoring and the website business assumes access to a car. If you don't have one, tutoring, the website business, and campus-walkable storefront routes are the only realistic options.
5. **Burnout and grades.** Hard cap: **15 hrs/week per partner during the semester** and 5 hrs/week during finals. Plan growth for breaks.

### Did the review change the ranking?
- **Order unchanged** (the top three remain within 0.05 points of each other).
- **Changes made:** (1) reduced the window-cleaning base case after the forum evidence; (2) tied the pet-waste recommendation to a **spring launch**; (3) made window cleaning's "Best Overall" status **conditional on a retail-density check** (≥40 independent storefronts within 10 minutes of campus); (4) tutoring repositioned as a **hedge and hourly benchmark**, with the agency step gated by a waitlist test.
- **If your campus fails the retail-density check:** switch the order. **Pet waste becomes #1** (pre-sell now, launch in March), and tutoring covers cash flow until then.
